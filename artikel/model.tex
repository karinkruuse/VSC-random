\section{Noise Contributions}
\label{sec:performance}

The optical extension adds modulation and detector noise to the electronic testbed. We estimate these contributions, combine them with the measured electronic baseline and an assumed board-timing spectrum, and propagate them through TDI with changing arm lengths. The result describes the included noise sources. It is not a prediction of the complete testbed performance, since work on the delay implementation is ongoing.

\subsection{Laser and Clock Noise}
% since these are assumed to get perfectly cancelled, why include them? it only makes sense to include them if we model the ranging errors

% we adapt it for modelling. But also, we dont actually model any noise herer. so like why include this at all? we already said that we will aim for this noise level.
For the laser frequency noise we adopt the LISA pre-stabilization reference \cite{bayle_unified_2023},
\begin{equation}
 A_\nu(f)=30\,\frac{\mathrm{Hz}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Hz}}}
 \sqrt{1+\left(\frac{2\,\mathrm{mHz}}{f}\right)^4}.
\end{equation}
For the spacecraft clocks we use the LISA fractional-frequency noise model \cite{hartwig_clock-jitter_2021},
\begin{equation}
 S_{\dot q}(f)=4\times10^{-27}\left(\frac{f}{1\,\mathrm{Hz}}\right)^{-1}\mathrm{Hz}^{-1}.
\end{equation}
The timing-noise ASD is $\sqrt{S_{\dot q}}/(2\pi f)$. The experiment will be configured to reproduce this clock-noise level over the analysis band. It refers to the timing shared by each spacecraft's modulation and phase measurement, not to the separate delay-board jitter $\epsilon_i$.

These are the primary noises that TDI and clock correction are intended to suppress. The calculation below assumes that the processing delays match the intended physical delays. It does not use these input levels to predict residuals from the current hardware's delay errors.

\subsection{Modulation and Optical Readout Noise}
\label{sec:readout_noise}

The modulation measurement takes half the phase difference between the upper and lower sidebands of a local optical beat. This removes the common optical phase and retains modulation fluctuations together with noise added by the readout.
%no, we assume it to be fully direct pahse noise currently.
We use the present spectrum as an estimate of the source modulation noise $m_i$.


Shot noise, dark-current shot noise, thermal noise and additive intensity noise are converted into phase noise by dividing by the beat amplitude. For a locally white current-noise ASD $i_n$ and peak beat current $I_{\mathrm{pk}}$,
\begin{equation}
 A_\phi=\frac{\sqrt{2}\,i_n}{2\pi I_{\mathrm{pk}}}.
 \label{eq:perf_phase_asd}
\end{equation}
The factor $\sqrt{2}$ accounts for noise on both sides of the RF beat; $2\pi$ converts radians to cycles. With an ideal three-way electrical split and a 14-bit, $2\,\mathrm{GS/s}$ ADC, the calculated carrier and sideband readout ASDs are $3.40\times10^{-9}$ and $1.24\times10^{-8}$ cycles/$\sqrt{\mathrm{Hz}}$. The ADC sampling rate here is distinct from the 125 MHz digital processing rate. Figure~\ref{fig:newport_readout} shows the contributions.

These small values follow from the strong laboratory beats and ideal readout assumptions. They are not measurements of the complete receiver. In particular, RF losses, excess ADC noise and imperfect filtering can raise the readout contribution.

miniLISA does not need to reproduce LISA's weak received light or its shot-noise-limited readout. Stronger signals give a lower detector contribution and make residual laser and clock noise easier to study. With the present inputs, the electronic and modulation spectra exceed the calculated detector noise.

Detector noise added before the electrical split is shared between boards. It must therefore be propagated as a shared input, while noise added after the split is assigned to the individual readouts. This differs from adding an independent detector noise to every LISA link.

\subsection{Electronic Noise and Board Timing}
\label{sec:electronic_noise}

The electronic delay line and its timing-noise coupling were studied by Ferguson et al.~\cite{ferguson2025applying}. We retain the measured electronic baseline as an input to the optical-testbed calculation. The same baseline PSD is assigned to carrier and sideband readouts, with independent equivalent noise terms for each link and channel. This represents the measured chain without separating every electronic component. The ideal ADC estimate above is not added again to the combined curve.

Development of the delay line is ongoing. The additive baseline and the coupling of board timing remain relevant when optical inputs are introduced, so both are included here. Other residuals associated with the present delay implementation are being addressed separately. Their eventual reduction has not been demonstrated by this calculation.

For board timing only, we use the Rb-standard model: a 10 MHz reference with single-sideband phase noise of $-130\,\mathrm{dBc/Hz}$ at 10 Hz \cite{srs_rb_standard}. Below 10 Hz, its phase PSD is extrapolated as $f^{-1}$ down to 1 Hz, $f^{-2}$ down to 10 mHz, and $f^{-3}$ below 10 mHz. This gives a timing ASD of $2.25\,\mathrm{ps}/\sqrt{\mathrm{Hz}}$ at 10 mHz. It is an assumed board-clock spectrum, separate from the LISA spacecraft-clock model above.

The plotted board contribution assumes independent $\epsilon_i$ and applies no additional board-timing correction. A fluctuation shared by all boards cancels to the same order as laser noise in the ideal model; differences between boards can remain. The actual contribution therefore depends on the clock distribution. Board timing also changes the physical delay, as described in Section~\ref{sec:delay_line}. Its nominal beat-frequency coupling is already included in the $\epsilon_i$ terms and is not added again as independent ranging noise. The coupling of delay fluctuations to laser-frequency noise is not included in this component sum.

\subsection{Propagation Through TDI with Changing Arms}
\label{sec:noise_propagation}
% This section misses the point. all that needs to be axplained here is the method of propagating the noises though TDI. and we basically do the same as the clock jitter paper by Hartwig and Bayle.

We use the four links of the second-generation Michelson observable $X_2$. Every delay is evaluated at the time reached after the preceding delays. For example,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal D_{12}\mathcal D_{21}x(t)
 =x\!\left(t-d_{12}(t)-d_{21}(t-d_{12}(t))\right).
\end{equation}
The calculation retains this ordering rather than multiplying the first-generation result by a static filter.

% Some of this, like the frequencies, should be in the appendix maybe?
The example uses $d_{12}(t)=d_{21}(t)=L+vt$ and $d_{13}(t)=d_{31}(t)=L-vt$, with $L=8.339\,\mathrm s$ and $v=5\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{s/s}$. One arm grows while the other shrinks. This is a simple flexing-arm example, not an orbit reconstruction. The nominal electrical input frequencies are $(15.0,10.9,21.6)\,\mathrm{MHz}$ and the modulation frequencies are $(35,36,34)\,\mathrm{MHz}$. The beat-frequency coefficients are held constant to isolate the effect of changing delays.

For this reduced measurement model, $\alpha_{j1}=-\alpha_{1j}$. The clock terms cancel in the corrected round trips
\begin{equation}
 R^{\mathrm c}_{1j}=\eta_{1j}+\mathcal D_{1j}\eta_{j1}-\alpha_{1j}r_{1j},
 \qquad j=2,3.
\end{equation}
The corrected second-generation combination is then
\begin{align}
 X_{2\mathrm c}={}&(1-\mathcal D_{121}-\mathcal D_{12131}
                  +\mathcal D_{1312121})R^{\mathrm c}_{13}\nonumber\\
 &-(1-\mathcal D_{131}-\mathcal D_{13121}
                  +\mathcal D_{1213131})R^{\mathrm c}_{12}.
 \label{eq:performance_x2}
\end{align}
Successive subscripts specify the ordered link path, for example $\mathcal D_{12131}=\mathcal D_{12}\mathcal D_{21}\mathcal D_{13}\mathcal D_{31}$. This is the phase-domain Michelson construction of \cite{hartwig_clock-jitter_2021}. It cancels laser noise through first order in the arm-length rates. The simple round-trip clock correction used here relies on constant, opposite forward and reverse beat frequencies; an orbit-dependent frequency plan requires the corresponding general clock correction.

Each noise source is inserted into the carrier and sideband measurements before forming Eq.~\eqref{eq:performance_x2}. All appearances of one source are combined before calculating its noise power. This retains the correlations of shared detector inputs and of the carrier readout reused in clock correction. Independent source powers are then added. Four independent noises with unit weights would give twice the single-source ASD; the actual TDI weights depend on frequency.

For the spectral curves, we evaluate the response of each ordered path at 25 times spanning one day and average the local output powers. This uses the slow variation of the delays to estimate the component spectra; it is not a full time-domain simulation of an orbit or of the firmware. The calculation is checked against the static limit and the first-order laser-noise cancellation for unequal arm rates.
